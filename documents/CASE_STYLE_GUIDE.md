# Skyggespill — case-specific artifact style guide

Status: production baseline v0.1  
Specification: CER 2.0

## Design premise

Skyggespill must look like a heterogeneous case file assembled from real institutions, people, media and organisations. There is no global decorative “murder mystery” template. The case identity comes from curation, paper handling and recurring case packaging, while each source retains its own visual language.

The visual tone is contemporary Norwegian political and investigative realism: restrained, credible and recognisably ordinary. Avoid cinematic spy aesthetics, red-string conspiracy graphics, fake classified stamps, typewriter clichés, excessive grunge and escape-room visual codes.

## Standard page format

Normal text-bearing documents are A4 portrait unless their source naturally requires another format. A3 is reserved for material that genuinely benefits from spatial overview. Documents must remain readable when printed at 100% on ordinary home/office printers.

Critical evidence must survive modest printer variation, grayscale where the source permits it, and normal viewing distance. No critical clue may depend only on subtle colour difference, tiny footer text or high-resolution zoom.

## Source identities

### Police / official investigation
Clean administrative layout, restrained hierarchy, ordinary case identifiers, factual captions and practical tables. Scene-photo sheets may use numbered images and short factual captions. Do not make police material look secret or dramatic.

### Hotel
Commercial operations material: access extracts, room/floor plan, booking/operations typography. It should not resemble police documents. Floor geometry must be immediately understandable.

### Nystart
Young contemporary political organisation. Competent but not over-polished establishment branding. Materials may feel energetic, digital-first and campaign-oriented. Avoid visual coding that tells players the party is sinister.

### Fremtidspartiet / Martin public material
More conventional established-party and national-media presentation. Political difference should be legible through content and branding, not moral colour coding.

### Media
Different outlets must look genuinely different. Breaking-news LDOC-14 should provide a strong visual phase break. Do not reuse one fake-news template across all clippings.

### Conference
Professional European policy/security event identity. Neutral international institutional style. Photography should plausibly be event documentation, not staged suspect portraits.

### Ingrid personal material
Calendar, notes and annotations should feel functional and used. Her handwriting/marking system must remain consistent. Annotations are reasoning traces, not puzzle highlighting.

### Martin personal material
Handwritten working notes should be visibly distinct from Ingrid's handwriting and annotation habits. They should show hypothesis evolution, crossings-out, arrows and questions, but remain readable.

### Business / supplier records
LDOC-20 deliberately contains heterogeneous documents. Each company gets its own mundane commercial identity. True provenance relationships may repeat job references, contact details or production credits; unrelated documents must not accidentally share templates, logos or metadata conventions.

## Photography

Photos should resemble the source that plausibly created them: police documentation, conference/event photography, press photography, service/workshop documentation or ordinary phone imagery. Avoid uniformly cinematic lighting.

Recurring people use canonical visual references. Identity-critical Morozov images in LDOC-07 and LDOC-16 must remain recognisable across age-appropriate context, angle and lighting without being identical copies.

## Evidence emphasis

Do not visually highlight clues merely because they matter to the solution. A clue receives emphasis only when the originating person or institution had a reason to mark it. Ingrid's marks in LDOC-17 are legitimate because Ingrid herself was comparing the material. The supplier credit in LDOC-10 must remain mundane because Thomas had no reason to flag it.

## Phase contrast

Phase 1 should feel like review of a closed political death investigation: orderly, finite, documentary. Phase 2 should initially feel disruptive and contemporary through LDOC-14, then become more personal and fragmented as players enter Martin's unfinished investigation.

The visual transition must not imply that phase 1 was fake. LDOC-13 should deliver genuine closure before phase 2 reopens context rather than culpability.

## Typography and layout

Use conventional readable sans/serif choices appropriate to each source. Avoid one font family across every fictional organisation. Body text should generally sit around normal 10–12 pt print equivalents. Logs/tables may be smaller but must remain comfortably readable.

Whitespace is preferable to artificial density. Long prose should be edited before shrinking type.

## Colour

Colour belongs to source identity, photography and real annotations. It is not a universal clue code. Any critical inference must still work without relying solely on colour.

## Paper realism

Moderate imperfections are allowed when source-driven: folds, scanner skew, photocopy softness, handwriting, clipped screenshots. Do not apply universal stains, torn edges or fake ageing to contemporary documents.

## Prohibited visual shortcuts

- red circles/arrows added by the game designer around hidden clues
- generic TOP SECRET or classified stamps without truth/provenance basis
- police evidence boards with string
- repeated fake document templates across unrelated sources
- tiny Easter-egg clues requiring magnification
- QR/password/cipher gates for ordinary discovery
- decorative coordinates, case numbers or timestamps that accidentally imply relationships
- AI-generated text embedded in images where exact text matters; exact text should be laid out as controlled typography

## Production QA

Every artifact must pass: source plausibility, actor knowledge boundary, provenance consistency, clue legibility, print readability, visual-source distinctiveness and solution-density review. Image-generation output is treated as raw visual material, never authoritative text. Exact names, dates, captions, logos and evidentiary wording are added in controlled layout.
